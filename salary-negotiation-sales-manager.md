## 2. Your real leverage isn't sales — it's the CRM

You're building reporting infrastructure a company has operated without. Two things are now true
that weren't six months ago: **the business can see itself**, and **you made that possible**.

1. **You hold knowledge nobody else has.** Whoever configures the CRM understands the commercial
   reality better than almost anyone — lead sources, conversion, where deals die.
2. **You have a sponsor.** A Business Advisor in an Australian SME almost always reports to the
   owner. Third-party validation of your scope is the most underused lever in an internal promotion.
3. **You already have the numbers.** You don't need to ask for build count and average contract
   value — you're building the system that reports them.
4. **Replacing you is genuinely hard.** Granny flat CDC knowledge *plus* the CRM build in one head
   is a combination they'd struggle to hire.

### ⚠️ The risk in how you're framing this

You described your goal as continuing to learn sales, train in construction, and improve processes.
Good career plan — **but don't lead with it in the salary conversation.** If you present yourself
as someone whose objective is to learn, a reasonable employer concludes they're already paying you
in opportunity. It's the most common way capable people in your exact position get underpaid.

Value first, development second: *"I've built X, it's produced Y, and I want to keep growing into Z."*
Same sentence, opposite outcome.

### Build your evidence pack

Things that couldn't be answered before you arrived and can be now:

- **Pipeline visibility** — "X active opportunities worth $Y at any moment." The headline.
- **Lead source and conversion** — if it changed marketing spend, say what it saved.
- **Lead response time** — easiest metric to move, most tied to revenue in high-ticket residential.
- **Follow-up leakage** — "X leads/month going cold." × average contract value = a very large number.
- **Forecasting** — for a builder booking trades and materials ahead, an operational saving.
- **Process work with the Business Advisor** — name specific processes with before/after numbers.

### Get the Business Advisor on side first

> *"I'm going to be discussing a move into a sales manager role. You've seen the process work up
> close — would you be comfortable sharing your view on it with the director?"*

You're not asking them to advocate a number. You're asking them to describe what you've done to
the person who decides.

### Price the whole job

The trap: **you take the sales manager title, and the CRM and process work quietly continues as
unpaid extra.** Get the scope named — either in the title (*Sales Manager (Sales Operations)*,
*Sales & Operations Manager*) or as explicit responsibilities in the job description. Written down,
it's scope you were paid for. Unwritten, it's a favour you keep doing.

## 3. The granny flat maths — and the trap

Granny flat turnkey contract: **$180,000–$260,000** (mid-range ~$200–220k).
Project home: $450,000–$700,000.

| Structure | Project home ($600k) | Granny flat ($200k) | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1% of contract *(industry standard)* | $6,000 | $2,000 | Same effort, a third of the reward |
| 1.5% of contract | $9,000 | $3,000 | Still below the flat-fee norm |
| **Flat $5–10k per sale** *(also standard)* | $5–10,000 | $5–10,000 | **2.5–5× better on a granny flat** |
| 2.5–3% of contract | — | $5–6,000 | The % that actually matches the flat fee |

> **The trap:** most granny flat builders inherited a percentage structure from project-home
> builders. Copied onto a $200,000 product it systematically underpays. Raising this is exactly
> what a sales manager is for — and your CRM data proves it. It also raises your own override.

### Modelling your override

~15 builds per consultant/year at $200,000 average contract:

| Team size | Builds/yr | Team revenue | Override @ 0.25% | Override @ 0.5% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 45 | $9.0M | $22,500 | $45,000 |
| **5** | **75** | **$15.0M** | **$37,500** | **$75,000** |
| 8 | 120 | $24.0M | $60,000 | $120,000 |

Flat $500/build = 0.25%; $1,000/build = 0.5%. **Run it on your real numbers — you have them.**

## 4. The market is moving your way

- **CDC pathway.** Housing SEPP 2021: secondary dwelling ≤60m² on a lot ≥450m² approved as
  complying development in as little as 20 days. ~85% of 2025 granny flat jobs used it.
- **Affordability crisis is the demand engine.** A granny flat adds $100–200k in property value
  plus rental income.
- **Available on almost every detached block**, including R2 — most of Western Sydney.
- **Specialist knowledge doesn't transfer in.** CDC timelines, site cost variation, investor-yield
  selling — let alone your CRM.

Don't say "the market is hot so pay me more." Say:

> *"The CDC pathway and the affordability picture mean this part of the business should keep
> growing — I'd like the role structured so I'm rewarded for growing it, not just maintaining it."*

## 5. Commission: what to accept, what to fight

| Component | How it works here | Typical weight |
|---|---|---|
| **Team override** | 0.25–0.5% of team contract value, or flat $500–1,000/build | 40–60% |
| **Personal sales** (player-coach) | You keep your leads and full consultant commission | 30–50% |
| **Volume / growth bonus** | Step bonus at build-count milestones — the accelerator conversation | 10–20% |
| **Margin protection** | Tied to gross margin, discourages discounting to close | 0–15% |

### Pay mix — correcting my earlier advice

I said push for 70/30. **In residential construction that's wrong and signals you don't know the
sector.** Consultants here sit at 50/50 or worse. The realistic manager ask is **60/40** — and the
non-commissionable ops half of your job lets you defend the fixed side better than most.

### The granny-flat-specific questions

1. **When does commission vest — signing, CDC approval, slab, or completion?** The most important
   question. Months pass between contract and slab; that's where these deals die.
2. **Cancellation rate between signing and slab?** Structurally high — clients sign, then discover
   site costs. *You can probably answer this from your own CRM.*
3. **Who wears site-cost variations?** Clay footings $8–16k, sloping sites $10–28k, service runs
   $4–9k. If deducted from commissionable value, your rate drops on the hardest sites.
4. **Contract value or gross margin?** Margin-based is fine only if you can see the margin.
5. **Clawback window post-slab?** Capped?
6. **Does the override count builds you sold personally?** It should.
7. **Accelerators above target?** The real upside in a growth market, and cheap for them to grant.
8. **Who owns lead allocation?** If you distribute leads, you control your own override.
9. **Can I see the plan in writing before accepting?** Non-negotiable.

## 6. Superannuation — your 12% is not a benefit

Since **1 July 2025** the minimum legal SG is **12%** — top of the phased schedule, no further
increases legislated. Your "$75,000 + 12% super" is the legal minimum.

> - `$120,000 + super` → **package $134,400**
> - `$120,000 package (inclusive)` → real base **$107,142** — nearly $13,000 less
>
> Ask: *"Is that plus super, or a total package including super?"*
> Then the one most people forget: *"Is super paid on commission as well as base?"*
> On a $60,000 commission year that's over $7,000.

## 7. The conversation, step by step

**1 · Open with what you've built, not what you want**
> *"Over the last few months I've put in the CRM processes we were missing — we can now see
> pipeline, lead source and conversion, which we couldn't before. I've also been working with
> [Business Advisor] on the process side. I'd like to talk about formalising that into a sales
> manager role."*

Note what's *not* in it: no "I've been learning a lot", no "I feel ready."

**2 · Settle the scope — including the half that isn't selling**
> *"Before we talk numbers — how many consultants would I be managing, do I keep my own leads,
> who owns lead allocation, and does CRM and process ownership stay with me? I want to make sure
> we're pricing the whole role."*

**3 · Anchor on both job families**
> *"Looking at the market, new home sales manager roles in Sydney advertise OTE north of $220,000,
> and sales operations managers sit around $135,000 base because that work isn't commissionable.
> This role is both. I'd be looking at $132,000 plus super, with an OTE around $200,000."*

**4 · Handle "but you're on $75,000 now"**
> *"I understand — but we're not adjusting my current salary, we're pricing a different role. If
> you hired this externally you'd pay market rate plus ramp-up, and here that ramp is longer than
> usual: they'd need to learn granny flat CDC processes and the CRM build from scratch."*

**5 · Raise the commission structure as a team issue**
> *"If the consultant plan is a percentage of contract value, it's paying materially less per sale
> than the industry flat-fee norm, because our contracts are $200,000 rather than $600,000. From
> the CRM data that's showing up in retention. I'd like to review it."*

**6 · If the base won't move, trade structure**
> *"If $132,000 isn't possible now, could we do $118,000 from day one with accelerators above the
> team target, and a written review at six months moving to $128,000 against agreed build numbers?
> I'd want those numbers defined now, in the letter."*

**7 · Now — and only now — the development conversation**
> *"Separately from the salary — I'd like to keep building the construction side of my knowledge.
> Could we include a training budget for a Cert IV in Building or an HIA course, and formalise the
> process work with [Business Advisor] as part of the role?"*

Asked after the money, it's a bonus. Asked before, it becomes the reason they don't pay you more.

**8 · Don't accept in the room**
> *"Could you put it in writing, including the commission plan? I'd like to review the details and
> come back to you within a couple of days."*

## 9. The most expensive mistakes

1. **Leading with wanting to learn.** It becomes the currency they pay you in.
2. **Letting the CRM and process work stay invisible.** Unwritten = a favour, not paid scope.
3. **Accepting the title now, money later.** Once you're doing the job, leverage is gone.
4. **Taking the OTE on faith.** You can check it — you built the reporting.
5. **Not asking when commission vests.** Signing vs completion can be $20,000/year.
6. **Accepting a percentage plan copied from project homes.** 1% of $200k is $2,000.
7. **Not clarifying inclusive vs. plus super, or super on commission.** Together, up to $20,000.
8. **Apologising for asking.** Say the number, stop talking, wait.

### Where this should land

**$118,000 base + super, CRM ownership written into the role, commission vesting at slab,
accelerators above target, car allowance and a training budget** — realistic first-year earnings
around **$160,000–180,000** against your current **$84,000** package.

The jump looks large because your current salary is low for what you're already doing, not because
the ask is aggressive. You are being paid as a sales assistant while implementing the company's
commercial infrastructure.
