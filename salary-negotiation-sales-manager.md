## 3. Commission — confirmation and nuance

**Your instinct is right but incomplete.** Yes, once you're a manager commission shifts to
depend on the team's target. But it's rarely only that — the Australian norm is a blend.

| Component | How it works | Typical weight of variable |
|---|---|---|
| **Team override** | Small % (≈2–4%) on all team revenue, or a bonus scaled to % of target | 50–70% |
| **Personal quota** (player-coach) | You keep your accounts and your commission. Very common in small teams | 20–40% |
| **Coaching / effectiveness** | Bonus on % of your team hitting quota. Rewards lifting weak performers | 0–15% |
| **MBO** | Non-revenue objectives: hiring, retention, pipeline coverage | 0–20% |

### The point almost nobody negotiates: *pay mix*

The base-to-variable ratio. An individual rep sits at 50/50 or 60/40.
**A manager should sit at 70/30 or 75/25.**

The argument, which works very well said out loud: *you no longer control the outcome directly,
you depend on other people selling. The less direct control you have over the number, the more
of your package should be fixed.*

> ⚠️ **Red flag:** if they offer to keep your current aggressive mix, or increase it "because you
> have more leverage now", they're transferring risk to you. It's where most people lose money
> on promotion.

### Nine questions before signing

An OTE of $150,000 is worth nothing if the team never hits target.

1. **What % of the team hit target in the last 4 quarters?** Under 60% and the OTE is fiction.
2. **Who sets the quota, and can it change mid-year?** If they can raise it in Q3, you don't have a plan.
3. **Is it uncapped? Accelerators above 100%?**
4. **Paid on booked, invoiced or collected revenue?** "Collected" can mean waiting 90 days.
5. **Clawback if a client cancels?** Over what window?
6. **If a rep leaves or a vacancy goes unfilled, is team quota pro-rated?** Critical — without it, someone else's turnover costs you money.
7. **Ramp or guarantee for the first 2 quarters?** You're inheriting a team, not starting from zero.
8. **Paid monthly, quarterly, annually?**
9. **Can I see the plan in writing before accepting?** If they hesitate, you have your answer.

## 4. Superannuation — your 12% is not a benefit

Since **1 July 2025** the minimum legal Superannuation Guarantee in Australia is **12%**. It's
the top of the phased schedule; no further increases are legislated.

Your "$75,000 + 12% super" is simply the legal minimum. It's not a perk of your package, and if
it's presented as one, that's a point in your favour.

> ⚠️ **Always check: *inclusive* vs. *plus***
>
> - `$120,000 + super` → you earn $120,000, company contributes $14,400 on top. **Package: $134,400**
> - `$120,000 package (inclusive of super)` → your actual base is **$107,142**. **Nearly $13,000 less**
>
> Ask literally: *"Is that plus super, or a total package including super?"*

## 5. The conversation, step by step

### 1. Separate the role from the salary — and do it first

Settle *what* the job includes: how many people, what quota, what budget, who you report to.
Without that you can't price it.

> *"Before we talk numbers, I'd like to make sure we're aligned on the scope — how many reps I'd
> be managing, what the team target looks like, and what I'd own beyond that. Then we can price
> the role properly."*

**Why it works:** positions you as someone thinking about the job, not their payslip.

### 2. Let them go first

Turn the question around **once**. Pushing twice reads as gamesmanship.

> *"I've done some research on the Sydney market, but you'll have a band in mind for this role —
> what range were you working with?"*

**Why it works:** their range usually starts higher than what you'd have asked for.

### 3. Anchor with a range, not a number

Build the range so **your real target is the floor**. A bare number can only come down.

> *"Based on SEEK and the Hays and Michael Page guides, sales manager roles in Sydney sit between
> $115,000 and $135,000 base plus super. Given I already know the product, the pipeline and the
> clients, I'd be looking at $120,000 plus super, with an OTE around $150,000."*

**Why it works:** the range comes from sources they consult too. Your $108–115k target sits
comfortably inside it.

### 4. Handle "but you're on $75,000 now"

It's coming. Have it answered word for word.

> *"I understand — but we're not adjusting my current salary, we're pricing a different role. If
> you hired this manager externally, you'd be paying market rate for it, plus six months of
> ramp-up. I'd like the role priced the same way."*

**Why it works:** the central reframe of the whole negotiation. Don't concede here.

### 5. If the budget constraint is real, negotiate structure

> *"If $120,000 isn't possible right now, could we do $105,000 from day one, with a written review
> at six months moving to $118,000 subject to agreed targets? I'd want those targets defined now,
> in the letter."*

**Critical:** "we'll review in six months" with no criteria and no written figure is **worth
nothing**. If it isn't in the offer letter, it doesn't exist.

### 6. Don't accept in the room

> *"Thank you — this sounds like a great step. Could you put it in writing, including the
> commission plan? I'd like to review the details and come back to you within a couple of days."*

## 6. If base stalls: other levers

| Lever | Value to you | Likelihood |
|---|---|---|
| **Management training budget** ($3–8k/yr) | Very high — first management role, and makes you more marketable after | High |
| **Written 6-month review** | High if it carries a figure and criteria; zero if not | High |
| **Guaranteed commission during ramp** (2 quarters) | High — protects income while you stabilise the team | Medium-high |
| **Formal title** ("Sales Manager", not "Team Lead") | Very high over time — sets your next band | High |
| **Sign-on bonus** | Medium — bridges the gap without touching base | Medium |
| **Flexibility / WFH days** | Variable | High |
| **Visa sponsorship / path to PR** | Enormous if it applies | Depends |

> **On the title:** accept "Team Leader" instead of "Sales Manager" only if it comes with manager
> money. The title is what the next recruiter reads and it determines the band you negotiate your
> next job from. A cheap title today costs you money for years.

## 8. The six most expensive mistakes

1. **Accepting the title now and discussing money later.** The most common tactic in internal
   promotions. Once you're doing the job, you lose all leverage.
2. **Negotiating against your current salary instead of the price of the job.**
3. **Focusing only on base and signing the commission plan unread.** A bad plan turns a $150,000
   OTE into $115,000 of actual income.
4. **Not clarifying whether the figure includes superannuation.** Up to $13,000 of difference.
5. **Apologising for asking.** No "I know this might be a lot, but…". Say the number, stop
   talking, wait. The silence works for you.
6. **Having no alternative.** Your real power is knowing what the market would pay you elsewhere.
   Never threaten to leave — but know it, because it shows in how you speak.
